**Summary**

ToxScreen is a benchmark of ~800 backdoored LLMs to study whether defenders (with white-box weight access but no training data or trusted model) can recover triggers. Gradient-based prompt optimization fails broadly; a token lookup ranked by attack success recovers triggers where backdoors are effective. The work analyzes mechanistic differences between backdoors and jailbreaks and releases models and code.

**Why it matters**

Directly relevant to model integrity for LLM inference in constrained settings. If you run LLMs under MPC/secure inference, detecting or recovering backdoor triggers without training data is crucial—especially when weights are available but evaluation is constrained by encryption/quantization.

**Relation to current work**

You can borrow the white-box detection strategies and benchmark methodology. In MPC contexts you might need to adapt detection into protocols that work with encrypted/obfuscated weights or combine token-lookup techniques with secure attestation/zero-knowledge proofs.

**Transferable ideas**

1) Integrate token-lookup + attack-success scoring into offline audits for protected inference pipelines; 2) design verifiable detection protocols that operate on encrypted weights (via MPC/Homomorphic ops) to test trigger candidates; 3) use high jailbreakability as an anomaly signal to trigger further audits or rollback.
