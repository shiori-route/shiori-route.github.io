**Summary**

Proposes Knowledge Access Planning (KAP), an IR-driven paradigm that compiles structured knowledge priors into runtime access plans to govern KV access during LLM serving without changing model weights; GraphSpec instantiation reduces decode-time KV access massively while preserving answer quality on long-context QA workloads.

**Why it matters**

Directly addresses runtime consumption inefficiencies by compiling knowledge-selection structure into physical access plans; this can substantially reduce KV I/O and thus is highly relevant for bandwidth- or crypto-constrained secure LLM inference and MPC runtimes.

**Relation to current work**

Highly relevant. KAP's plan-driven KV access could be applied to secure LLM inference stacks (MPC/HE) to reduce encrypted KV transfer, reduce rounds, and enable dependency-aware scheduling and protocol selection at decode time.

**Transferable ideas**

Potential transfers: 1) compile plans that specify minimal encrypted KV accesses to cut ciphertext traffic; 2) coalesce/reorder planned accesses to reduce communication rounds and WAN latency; 3) drive adaptive protocol selection per access block (e.g., use faster secret-sharing for hot items).

#### Deeper analysis (still title/abstract only)

**Research problem：** LLM serving backends observe only a flat token sequence once structured knowledge priors are serialized into prompts. This forces dense, uniform KV-state consumption during decoding, inflating memory/traffic and latency for long contexts even when only a small subset of the context is relevant—the Knowledge Selection-Runtime Consumption (KSRC) gap.

**Core method：** Introduce Knowledge Access Planning (KAP): elevate structured knowledge priors into first-class runtime artifacts (a runtime access plan IR) that govern physical KV access without altering prompt semantics or model weights. They instantiate KAP with GraphSpec (a compiler-executor) and derive a phase-boundary model characterizing the positive-speedup regime.

**Contributions**

- Formulate the KSRC (Knowledge Selection–Runtime Consumption) gap.
- Propose KAP and a runtime access plan IR to control physical KV access based on structured priors.
- Implement GraphSpec (compiler–executor) to decouple physical KV consumption from prompt length across long-context QA workloads.
- Derive a phase-boundary model for when plan-guided execution yields speedup.

**Transferable mechanisms**

- Compiling high-level selection signals into a runtime access plan IR—could govern when/how to fetch encrypted/shared data to reduce rounds.
- Decoupling physical data access from logical context length—helps selective reveal or on-demand computation in MPC.
- Phase-boundary performance model—to predict when plan-driven execution yields net gains under varying latency/bandwidth.
- Compiler–executor separation (GraphSpec)—eases inserting an access-planning layer into existing MPC runtimes.

**Potential overlap：** High overlap with MPC/PP inference goals of reducing data movement and on-demand access to large KV-states; shares techniques with runtime scheduling, batching and latency-hiding research.

**Potential conflict：** Shifting physical access while preserving logical semantics may introduce access-pattern side channels relevant to security/privilege contexts. The abstract does not indicate whether such leakage is analyzed or mitigated—requires full-text verification.

**Summary**

DraftExpert is an expansion-aware self-speculative decoding framework for end-device MoE inference where routed experts are offloaded. It trains one lightweight draft expert per layer via self-distillation of residuals, logits, and router agreement, and at inference uses shared+top-1+draft drafter, confidence-expansion truncation, and target-expert prefetching. Claims: ~1.45x throughput, high draft acceptance and prefetch hit rates across CPU-GPU and Flash-NPU setups.

**Why it matters**

Directly relevant to offload/prefetch problems: staged expert loading, speculative drafts, and prefetch strategies can inspire latency-hiding and resource-management techniques in secure LLM inference or MPC runtimes that stage heavy artifacts.

**Relation to current work**

Mechanisms to borrow: lightweight draft models to reduce load-triggered transfers, confidence/expansion truncation for conservative prefetching, and speculatively verifying final tokens—applicable to MPC runtimes that stage preprocessing, garbled circuits, or model shards.

**Transferable ideas**

Potential transfers: use lightweight local drafts to predict which heavy artifacts to stage in MPC (e.g., preprocessing, circuit fragments), apply confidence-based truncation to limit prefetch expansion, and keep exact verification on the critical path.

**Questions to verify**

- Does training lightweight draft experts access or expose target experts' sensitive weights/data?
- How are prefetch and caching secured when memory is encrypted or in regulated environments?
- What is the overhead (network/IO and verification time) when speculation fails?
